# China · K1 Overview

## 1. Country Character

China is the country children live in, so this unit should begin from familiar life rather than distant facts. For K1 children, China can be introduced through colours, celebration, city images, school life, daily food, family routines, and classroom participation. The country should feel close, warm, visible, and personal: the place of my school, my home, my city, my food, and my celebration.

The China unit should not become a formal lesson about geography, history, politics, or national knowledge. It should help young children notice that China is not only a word on a map, but also the environment they experience every day. Red and gold colours, stars, familiar city buildings, school photos, bowls, chopsticks, dumplings, noodles, and class-made decorations can all become concrete entry points.

## 2. Grade-Level Lens

For K1 children, China should be approached through personal, sensory, visual, and daily-life experiences. Children do not need to memorise information about China. They should see colours and symbols, recognise familiar places, touch and arrange celebration materials, play with food props, build simple city scenes, and contribute to a class display.

The main K1 lens is: **“This is where I live, go to school, eat, play, and celebrate.”**

K1 children may respond through looking, pointing, choosing, matching, moving, placing objects, making simple artwork, or using single words and short phrases. Verbal explanation should not be required.

## 3. Classroom Setup and Key Elements

The classroom can include a simple China corner with red and gold decorations, stars, small flags, photos of Shenzhen, photos of EASTIE, familiar city buildings, children’s drawings, food props, bowls, chopsticks, and a shared celebration wall. The setup should feel like a lived and shared environment, not an information board.

Key elements can include:

- red and gold colours
- stars and simple flag imagery
- National Day decorations
- Shenzhen city images
- EASTIE / school photos
- classroom and family-life images
- rice, noodles, dumplings, buns, bowls, and chopsticks
- children’s own artwork for a China celebration wall

For K1, large images, real or pretend objects, simple textures, safe props, and child-made display pieces are more useful than written facts, province names, or detailed maps.

## 4. Four-Lesson Map

### Lesson 1: China Colours and National Day

Children meet China through red, gold, stars, flags, and National Day decorations. They notice colours and shapes, wave small flags, match stars, and help decorate a China celebration corner.

This lesson builds the first visual and festive entry point into the China unit.

### Lesson 2: My City, My School, My China

Children connect China with their own lived environment: Shenzhen, EASTIE, the classroom, familiar buildings, and the journey to school. They look at photos, match school and city images, and build a simple city scene.

This lesson helps children understand China as the place where their everyday life happens.

### Lesson 3: Chinese Food in Daily Life

Children explore familiar Chinese daily foods through pretend play, pictures, food props, and simple sensory observation. They use bowls, chopsticks, plates, food cards, and role-play materials to create a shared mealtime scene.

This lesson connects China with care, family life, and familiar food routines.

### Lesson 4: My China Celebration Wall

Children bring together the K1 China experience by creating a class wall or display. They add colours, stars, school or city drawings, food images, class photos, and small decorations.

This lesson gives children a visible shared outcome and lets them point to, choose, and share what they made.
