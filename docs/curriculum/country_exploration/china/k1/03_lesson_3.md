# China · K1 · Lesson 3
## Chinese Food in Daily Life

## 1. Lesson Identity

- Country / Place: China
- Grade: K1
- Lesson: 3 of 4

## 2. Lesson Focus

Children explore familiar Chinese daily foods through pretend play, food props, table-setting, and simple sensory observation.

## 3. Learning Objectives

Children will:

- recognise familiar Chinese food and mealtime objects;
- sort, choose, serve, or arrange food props in a shared table scene;
- participate in pretend mealtime play and connect China with daily care, food, and familiar routines.

## 4. Teacher Language

Teacher Input:

- Look at the food.
- This is rice.
- This is noodles.
- This is a dumpling.
- This is a bowl.
- These are chopsticks.
- Put it in the bowl.
- Let’s eat.
- Do you like noodles?
- Here you are.
- Thank you.

Possible Child Response:

- rice
- noodles
- dumpling
- bun
- bowl
- chopsticks
- I like noodles.
- Here you are.
- Thank you.

Children may also respond by choosing food, serving food, placing items, pretending to eat, or pointing to preferred food.

## 5. Suggested Games and Activities

### Activity 1: Food Basket Sorting

**Purpose:**  
Help children recognise familiar Chinese foods and mealtime objects through sorting and choosing.

**Materials:**  
Pretend food or picture cards: rice, noodles, dumplings, buns, vegetables, soup; bowls, spoons, safe training chopsticks, trays or baskets.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare two or three simple baskets, such as “food,” “bowls,” and “tools.” If labels are used, rely on pictures rather than text.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Show the food props one by one. Children name, point, or repeat if ready. Then invite children to sort the items into baskets. The teacher uses simple language: “This is rice,” “Put it here,” “Bowl,” “Food,” and “Chopsticks.”

After sorting, children choose one food item they like and place it on a shared table mat.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Use familiar foods from children’s daily life. Avoid introducing too many regional dishes in one lesson.

### Activity 2: Set the Table

**Purpose:**  
Connect food with care, family life, and daily mealtime routines.

**Materials:**  
Small bowls, plates, spoons, child-safe chopsticks, placemats, pretend food, tablecloth or table mat.

**Preparation:**  
Set up a low table or floor mat as a shared mealtime area. Put food props and tableware in separate baskets.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Model setting one place: bowl, spoon, chopsticks, food. Invite children to help set the table. Some children place bowls, some place food, and some serve pretend noodles or dumplings.

Use repeated social language: “Here you are,” “Thank you,” “Let’s eat,” and “Do you like noodles?”

**Teacher Notes:**  
This is a practical-life and pretend-play activity. The aim is participation and routine, not correct table etiquette.

### Activity 3: Dumpling Shop Pretend Play

**Purpose:**  
Give children a simple role-play context for Chinese food and social interaction.

**Materials:**  
Playdough or soft pretend dumplings, trays, baskets, plates, aprons if available, simple shop sign, toy cash register if available.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare a small “dumpling shop” area. Roll simple playdough dumplings in advance or allow children to make them with teacher support.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children take turns being shop helpers and customers. Shop helpers place dumplings on plates or trays. Customers choose and receive food. The teacher models simple phrases: “Dumpling, please,” “Here you are,” and “Thank you.”

Children can add a photo or drawing of the dumpling shop to the China celebration wall.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Keep the role play simple. For K1, one exchange is enough: choose → receive → thank.
