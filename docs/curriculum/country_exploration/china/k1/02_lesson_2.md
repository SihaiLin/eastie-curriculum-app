# China · K1 · Lesson 2
## My City, My School, My China

## 1. Lesson Identity

- Country / Place: China
- Grade: K1
- Lesson: 2 of 4

## 2. Lesson Focus

Children connect China with their own everyday environment: Shenzhen, EASTIE, the classroom, familiar buildings, streets, and the journey to school.

## 3. Learning Objectives

Children will:

- recognise that China includes familiar places such as their city, school, and classroom;
- notice simple city and school images through looking, pointing, and matching;
- build or arrange a simple city-and-school scene and experience China as the place where their own daily life happens.

## 4. Teacher Language

Teacher Input:

- This is China.
- This is Shenzhen.
- This is our school.
- Look at the building.
- It is tall.
- This is a road.
- Let’s build a city.
- Put the school here.
- Where is our school?
- Here it is.

Possible Child Response:

- school
- city
- building
- road
- tall
- here
- my school
- I see school.
- I see a building.

Children may also point to photos, choose familiar images, move vehicles, or place a school card in the city scene.

## 5. Suggested Games and Activities

### Activity 1: Photo Walk — Our China

**Purpose:**  
Help children connect the word China with familiar places in their own life.

**Materials:**  
Printed photos of EASTIE, classroom, school gate, Shenzhen buildings, parks, roads, and familiar school surroundings.

**Preparation:**  
Place 5–8 large photos around the classroom or on a low display wall. Include at least two very familiar school photos so children can recognise them easily.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Walk with children from photo to photo. Ask them to look and point. Use simple teacher language: “This is our school,” “This is Shenzhen,” “This is a building,” and “This is China.” Children can choose a favourite photo or stand next to the image they recognise.

End by placing the school photo in the China corner.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Start with the most familiar photos before showing broader city images. Do not turn this into a map or city-name lesson.

### Activity 2: Build Our School in the City

**Purpose:**  
Allow children to represent familiar city and school life through construction play.

**Materials:**  
Large wooden blocks, Lego or magnetic blocks, toy cars, road mats or paper roads, printed school card, simple building picture cards.

**Preparation:**  
Set up a small building area with blocks and a simple road. Prepare one card labelled or pictured as “our school.”

**How to Play / Do:**  
Invite children to build tall and short buildings. Add roads and toy cars. The teacher then introduces the school card and asks, “Where is our school?” Children choose a place to put the school in the city.

The class can then move toy cars along the roads and say or hear simple phrases such as “Go to school,” “Stop,” “Road,” “Building,” and “Here is school.”

**Teacher Notes:**  
The building does not need to look realistic. The goal is to help children connect school, city, and China through play.

### Activity 3: Home-to-School Route Play

**Purpose:**  
Connect China with the child’s daily routine of coming to school.

**Materials:**  
Toy cars, buses, small people figures, paper roads, school picture card, simple home cards or block houses.

**Preparation:**  
Create a simple route on the floor or table: home → road → school. Keep the setup small enough for children to understand.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children choose a toy person or vehicle and move it from home to school. The teacher narrates with simple language: “Go to school,” “On the road,” “Here is school,” and “Good morning.” Children may repeat words, move the vehicle, wave, or place the person in the school.

After several turns, invite children to add the route or a photo to the China display.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Avoid asking children for detailed home information. Keep the activity general and routine-based.
