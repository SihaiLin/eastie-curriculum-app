# China · K1 · Lesson 1
## China Colours and National Day

## 1. Lesson Identity

- Country / Place: China
- Grade: K1
- Lesson: 1 of 4

## 2. Lesson Focus

Children meet China through red, gold, stars, flags, and National Day decorations. The focus is visual, sensory, and celebratory rather than informational.

## 3. Learning Objectives

Children will:

- notice red, gold, and star shapes as part of the China celebration environment;
- point to, match, place, or arrange simple China-related visual symbols;
- participate in decorating a small China corner or celebration space and experience China as a familiar class celebration.

## 4. Teacher Language

Teacher Input:

- Look. Red and gold.
- I see a star.
- This is China.
- This is a flag.
- Let’s decorate.
- Put the star here.
- Do you like red?
- Wave the flag.
- Happy National Day.

Possible Child Response:

- red
- gold
- star
- flag
- China
- here
- I see a star.
- I like red.

Children may also respond by pointing, waving, choosing, placing, or matching. Full verbal output should not be required.

## 5. Suggested Games and Activities

### Activity 1: Red and Gold Treasure Hunt

**Purpose:**  
Help children notice the main visual colours of the China celebration environment.

**Materials:**  
Red and gold paper, fabric pieces, cards, ribbons, stars, baskets or trays.

**Preparation:**  
Place red and gold items around the classroom at child height. Prepare two baskets or trays: one for red items and one for gold items.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Invite children to walk around the classroom and find red or gold objects. Children pick up one item at a time and place it in the matching basket. The teacher repeats simple language such as “red,” “gold,” “I see red,” and “Put it here.”

After collecting, children sit together and look at the baskets. The teacher briefly reviews the colours and connects them to the China celebration corner.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Keep the pace slow and concrete. For children who are not ready to name colours, accepting pointing, holding, or placing is enough.

### Activity 2: Star Match and Place

**Purpose:**  
Help children recognise stars as a repeated visual element in the China theme.

**Materials:**  
Large star cut-outs, small star stickers, red background paper, trays, glue sticks or removable adhesive.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare a large red background sheet for the class. Place star shapes in a basket. If using glue, pre-mark simple placement areas to reduce waiting time.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Show children one large star and say, “I see a star.” Invite each child to choose a star and place it on the red background. Children can match big and small stars, count a few stars if appropriate, or simply place one star on the display.

The finished red-and-star paper can become the first piece of the China celebration corner.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Do not explain national symbolism in detail. The aim is visual recognition and participation, not symbolic memorisation.

### Activity 3: Flag Wave and Celebration Walk

**Purpose:**  
Use movement to make the China celebration theme active and memorable.

**Materials:**  
Small safe paper flags or red-gold ribbons, simple music if available, open classroom or theatre space.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare enough small flags or ribbons for children. Clear a simple walking path. Avoid sticks or sharp flag holders for K1.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Give each child a small flag or ribbon. Model slow waving, high waving, low waving, and walking together. Use repeated language: “Wave the flag,” “Walk,” “Stop,” “Red,” “Gold,” and “Happy National Day.”

After the movement, children return their flags or ribbons to the China corner and help arrange them in a basket or display container.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Keep movement safe and controlled. The activity should feel like a gentle class celebration, not a performance drill.
