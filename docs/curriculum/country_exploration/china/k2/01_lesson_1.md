# China · K2 · Lesson 1
## Mountains, Rivers and Landscapes

## 1. Lesson Identity

- Country / Place: China
- Grade: K2
- Lesson: 1 of 4

## 2. Lesson Focus

Children explore different natural landscapes in China, such as mountains, rivers, forests, bamboo, rice fields, grasslands, and snowy places, through sorting, comparing, and simple landscape building.

## 3. Learning Objectives

Children will:

- recognise several different kinds of natural landscapes in China;
- sort and compare landscape images by visible features such as water, trees, fields, snow, and mountains;
- build or arrange a simple landscape scene and understand that China has many different natural environments.

## 4. Teacher Language

Teacher Input:

- Look at the mountain.
- This is a river.
- This is a forest.
- I see water.
- I see trees.
- Is it the same or different?
- Put the river here.
- Put the mountain here.
- Where is the forest?
- There are many places in China.

Possible Child Response:

- mountain
- river
- forest
- bamboo
- field
- snow
- same
- different
- I see water.
- It is a mountain.
- Put it here.

Children may also respond by pointing, sorting, matching, placing, building, or choosing between two landscape cards.

## 5. Suggested Games and Activities

### Activity 1: Landscape Picture Sort

**Purpose:**  
Help children notice and classify different Chinese landscapes by visible features.

**Materials:**  
Large picture cards of mountains, rivers, forests, bamboo, rice fields, grasslands, snowy mountains, and wetlands; baskets or sorting mats.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare two to four simple sorting mats, such as “water,” “trees,” “fields,” and “snow/mountains.” Use pictures rather than written labels.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Show one landscape picture at a time and invite children to say what they notice: water, trees, green, snow, mountain, field. Then ask children to place each picture on a matching sorting mat. Some pictures may fit more than one group, which can lead to simple comparison.

After sorting, review the mats and say: “China has mountains,” “China has rivers,” “China has fields,” and “China has forests.”

**Teacher Notes:**  
Do not require accurate place names. The focus is noticing landscape types and comparing visible features.

### Activity 2: Make a Mountain and River Scene

**Purpose:**  
Let children represent natural landscapes with concrete materials.

**Materials:**  
Blocks, blue fabric or paper strips, green paper or fabric, stones or loose parts, cardboard, animal or tree props if available.

**Preparation:**  
Set up a floor or table area with separate material baskets: blocks for mountains, blue pieces for rivers, green pieces for forests or fields.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Model making a simple scene: stack blocks for a mountain, lay blue fabric for a river, and place green pieces beside it. Invite children to work in small groups to build their own mountain-river scene.

Use prompts such as “Where is the river?” “Put trees here,” and “Is the mountain tall or short?” Children can walk around and look at each group’s scene.

**Teacher Notes:**  
For K2, encourage children to make choices and explain simply. Avoid correcting the scene for geographical accuracy.

### Activity 3: Same or Different Landscape Walk

**Purpose:**  
Support K2 comparison skills through movement and visual choice.

**Materials:**  
Pairs of landscape images, such as mountain/mountain, river/river, mountain/river, forest/field, snow/grassland; floor markers or hoops.

**Preparation:**  
Place one image at each station. Prepare a few image pairs to show children during the game.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Show two pictures and ask, “Same or different?” Children answer with words, gestures, or by moving to a “same” or “different” spot. Then ask one simple follow-up: “Where is water?” “Where are trees?” “Which one has snow?”

Repeat with several pairs. End by placing all pictures together and saying that China has many different places.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Use visual comparison rather than abstract explanation. Some images can be partly similar and partly different; accept reasonable child observations.
