# China · K2 · Lesson 2
## Animals of China and Their Homes

## 1. Lesson Identity

- Country / Place: China
- Grade: K2
- Lesson: 2 of 4

## 2. Lesson Focus

Children explore selected animals associated with China and connect them with simple habitats, such as bamboo forest, wetland, forest, mountain, or grassland.

## 3. Learning Objectives

Children will:

- recognise several animals associated with China;
- match animals with suitable habitats using picture cards or small-world scenes;
- compare animals by movement, body features, and homes, and understand that different animals need different places to live.

## 4. Teacher Language

Teacher Input:

- This is a panda.
- This is a crane.
- This is a monkey.
- Where does it live?
- It lives in the forest.
- It lives near water.
- Match the animal and the home.
- Is it big or small?
- Can it climb?
- Can it fly?
- Put the panda in the bamboo.

Possible Child Response:

- panda
- crane
- monkey
- tiger
- snow leopard
- bamboo
- forest
- water
- mountain
- It can climb.
- It can fly.
- Panda in bamboo.

Children may also respond by matching cards, moving like animals, placing animal models, or choosing a habitat.

## 5. Suggested Games and Activities

### Activity 1: Animal and Habitat Match

**Purpose:**  
Help children connect animals with homes instead of learning animals as isolated vocabulary.

**Materials:**  
Animal cards or models: panda, red-crowned crane, golden monkey, snow leopard, tiger; habitat cards or mats: bamboo forest, wetland, forest, mountain, grassland.

**Preparation:**  
Place habitat mats on the floor or table. Keep animal cards in a basket. Use clear, child-friendly pictures.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Show each animal and invite children to notice one feature: “It is black and white,” “It has long legs,” “It can climb,” “It is big.” Then children choose a habitat and place the animal there.

The teacher models simple relationships: “Panda in bamboo,” “Crane near water,” “Monkey in the forest.” Children repeat only if ready.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Keep the number of animals limited. It is better to use four or five animals meaningfully than to show too many.

### Activity 2: Move Like the Animal

**Purpose:**  
Use gross-motor movement to reinforce animal features and habitat ideas.

**Materials:**  
Animal picture cards, open space, optional floor markers for habitats.

**Preparation:**  
Place habitat markers around the room: bamboo, water, forest, mountain. Prepare animal cards for teacher prompts.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Hold up an animal card and invite children to move like the animal: panda walks slowly, crane steps carefully, monkey climbs or reaches, tiger walks quietly, snow leopard moves over mountains. Then children move to the matching habitat marker.

Use simple language: “Can it fly?” “Can it climb?” “Go to the forest,” “Go to the mountain.”

**Teacher Notes:**  
Set safe movement boundaries. The animal actions should be imaginative but controlled.

### Activity 3: Build an Animal Home

**Purpose:**  
Let children represent habitats using construction and loose parts.

**Materials:**  
Blocks, Lego, green paper, blue fabric, brown paper, paper tubes, small animal models or cards, leaves or natural-looking materials if available.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare small-group trays with one animal and a few habitat materials. Each group can build a simple home for one animal.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Each group receives one animal card or model. Children discuss where the animal should live and build a simple home. A panda group may make bamboo, a crane group may make water, and a snow leopard group may make mountains.

At the end, groups place their animal in the home and show one feature: “water,” “mountain,” “forest,” or “bamboo.”

**Teacher Notes:**  
The aim is relationship-building, not perfect habitat modelling. Accept simple symbolic representation.
