# China · K2 Overview

## 1. Country Character

China is the country children live in, but K2 children are ready to look beyond their immediate classroom, city, and family routines. In this unit, China can be introduced as a place with many different natural environments: mountains, rivers, bamboo forests, rice fields, tea hills, wetlands, grasslands, and snowy places. It can also be explored through animals, plants, farms, and the connection between nature and daily life.

The China unit should not become a geography memorisation lesson. Children do not need to learn province names, long map facts, or detailed regional information. Instead, they should notice that China has different kinds of places, and that different animals, plants, and foods belong to different environments. The unit should feel exploratory, visual, hands-on, and comparative.

## 2. Grade-Level Lens

For K2 children, China should be approached through sorting, comparing, matching, and making simple connections. Children can compare mountains and rivers, match animals with habitats, connect rice or tea with fields and farms, and organise different natural elements into a shared China landscape.

The main K2 lens is: **“China has many different natural places, and different living things belong in different places.”**

K2 children may respond through sorting cards, building scenes, matching animal homes, comparing same and different, placing items on a shared map, and using short phrases. They can begin to explain simple relationships, such as “panda in bamboo,” “rice in fields,” or “river and mountain.”

## 3. Classroom Setup and Key Elements

The classroom can include a Natural China corner with landscape images, animal cards, bamboo, rice-field pictures, tea-hill pictures, blue cloth for rivers, green fabric or paper for fields and forests, blocks for mountains, and a large shared floor or wall map. The setup should support comparison and organisation rather than display too many facts.

Key elements can include:

- mountains and rivers
- bamboo forests
- rice fields
- tea hills
- wetlands or ponds
- panda, red-crowned crane, golden monkey, snow leopard, or tiger
- farm and plant images
- a collaborative Natural China map

For K2, picture cards, loose parts, animal models, fabric, blocks, baskets, matching mats, and teacher-made map bases are more useful than written explanations or detailed map labels.

## 4. Four-Lesson Map

### Lesson 1: Mountains, Rivers and Landscapes

Children explore different Chinese landscapes through pictures, fabric, blocks, and sorting games. They notice mountains, rivers, forests, bamboo, rice fields, grasslands, and snowy places.

This lesson builds the idea that China has many different kinds of natural environments.

### Lesson 2: Animals of China and Their Homes

Children explore selected animals associated with China and match them to simple habitats. They compare animals by where they live, how they move, and what kind of environment they need.

This lesson helps children connect animals with habitats rather than treating animals as isolated pictures.

### Lesson 3: Farms, Rice Fields and Tea Hills

Children explore how nature connects with food and daily life. They look at rice fields, tea hills, vegetables, fruit, and simple farm scenes, then connect plants with foods or drinks they may know.

This lesson shifts from “food we eat” to “where some food comes from.”

### Lesson 4: Build a Natural China Map

Children bring together landscapes, animals, plants, and farms by building a large collaborative Natural China map. They place mountains, rivers, forests, fields, animals, and farms in a shared display.

This lesson helps children organise the unit’s ideas into one visible class project.
