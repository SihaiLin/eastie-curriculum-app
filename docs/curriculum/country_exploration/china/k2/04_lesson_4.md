# China · K2 · Lesson 4
## Build a Natural China Map

## 1. Lesson Identity

- Country / Place: China
- Grade: K2
- Lesson: 4 of 4

## 2. Lesson Focus

Children bring together landscapes, animals, plants, and farms by creating a collaborative Natural China map or display. The focus is organisation and simple relationships, not geographic accuracy.

## 3. Learning Objectives

Children will:

- recall and organise natural China elements from previous lessons;
- place landscapes, animals, plants, and farms onto a shared map or display;
- explain simple relationships such as animal-home or plant-food connections while contributing to a collaborative class project.

## 4. Teacher Language

Teacher Input:

- Let’s make a Natural China map.
- Where is the mountain?
- Put the river here.
- Put the panda in the bamboo.
- Rice grows in fields.
- Tea comes from leaves.
- What can you see?
- Is it the same or different?
- Tell me about your part.
- This is our map.

Possible Child Response:

- mountain
- river
- forest
- field
- panda
- crane
- rice
- tea
- Put it here.
- I see a river.
- Panda in bamboo.
- This is our map.

Children may also respond by placing cards, arranging materials, pointing to relationships, or showing one part of the map.

## 5. Suggested Games and Activities

### Activity 1: Natural China Review Basket

**Purpose:**  
Help children recall key elements before building the shared map.

**Materials:**  
A basket with landscape cards, animal cards, plant/food cards, and small loose parts from previous lessons.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare one mixed review basket. Include only items that have already appeared in K2 lessons.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children take turns pulling one item from the basket. They name it, point to a matching picture, or say where it belongs. For example, a panda card goes with bamboo, a river card goes near blue fabric, and rice goes with fields.

Use this review to prepare children for the collaborative map.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Accept nonverbal responses. The aim is recall and organisation, not testing.

### Activity 2: Build the Shared Natural China Map

**Purpose:**  
Let children organise the unit’s content into one collaborative visual structure.

**Materials:**  
Large floor paper, fabric, or wall display base; blue cloth or paper for rivers; blocks for mountains; green paper for forests and fields; animal cards or models; rice/tea/farm cards; tape or removable adhesive.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare a large base. Lightly mark a few open zones if needed, but do not make it too fixed. Put materials in category baskets: landscapes, animals, farms, plants.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Begin with the land and water: children place mountains, rivers, forests, and fields. Then they add animals and farms. Ask simple prompts: “Where does the panda live?” “Where is the river?” “Put rice in the field.”

Children work in small groups so each group contributes one part of the map. When complete, walk around the map together and review what is on it.

**Teacher Notes:**  
The map does not need to be geographically precise. It is a thinking map for K2 children to organise categories and relationships.

### Activity 3: Map Guide Walk

**Purpose:**  
Give children a simple sharing opportunity using pointing, short phrases, and group explanation.

**Materials:**  
Completed Natural China map, pointer sticks or soft pointers if available, name cards or group labels, camera for documentation.

**Preparation:**  
Choose two or three stopping points on the map: mountain/river, animal home, farm or tea hill. Prepare very simple prompts for each stop.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children walk around the map as a class. At each stop, one or two children point to an item and say a word or short phrase, such as “mountain,” “river,” “panda in bamboo,” or “rice field.” The teacher supports with sentence frames but does not force full sentences.

Take photos of the map and children’s pointing or sharing for classroom documentation.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Keep sharing short. K2 children should feel ownership of the map, not pressure to present formally.
