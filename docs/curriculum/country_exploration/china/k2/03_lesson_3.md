# China · K2 · Lesson 3
## Farms, Rice Fields and Tea Hills

## 1. Lesson Identity

- Country / Place: China
- Grade: K2
- Lesson: 3 of 4

## 2. Lesson Focus

Children explore how Chinese landscapes connect with food and daily life by looking at rice fields, tea hills, farms, plants, and simple plant-to-food relationships.

## 3. Learning Objectives

Children will:

- notice that some familiar foods and drinks come from plants and farms;
- match selected plants or field images with simple food or drink items;
- participate in pretend planting, harvesting, sorting, or farm-scene building and understand that nature, people’s work, and daily food can be connected.

## 4. Teacher Language

Teacher Input:

- This is a rice field.
- This is tea.
- Rice grows in fields.
- Tea comes from leaves.
- Put the rice here.
- Match the plant and the food.
- What can you see?
- I see green fields.
- First, plant. Then, pick.
- Thank you, farmers.

Possible Child Response:

- rice
- field
- tea
- leaves
- farm
- plant
- pick
- green
- I see rice.
- Rice in the field.
- Tea leaves.

Children may also respond by planting, scooping, matching cards, placing loose parts, or arranging a farm scene.

## 5. Suggested Games and Activities

### Activity 1: Plant to Food Match

**Purpose:**  
Help children make simple connections between plants, farms, and familiar food.

**Materials:**  
Picture cards: rice field, rice bowl, tea leaves, tea cup, vegetable garden, vegetables, fruit tree, fruit; baskets or matching mats.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare simple pairs. Avoid too many crops. Use no more than four plant-to-food pairs for K2.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Show a field or plant picture and ask, “What can you see?” Then show the related food or drink picture. Children match the pair and place them together. Use repeated language such as “rice field and rice,” “tea leaves and tea,” “plant and food.”

After matching, children can choose one pair and place it on a shared “farm to table” mat.

**Teacher Notes:**  
This is not a full agriculture lesson. Keep the relationship concrete: plant/field → food/drink.

### Activity 2: Rice Field Pretend Planting

**Purpose:**  
Give children a body-based and sensory-rich way to experience farming routines.

**Materials:**  
Brown or green floor mat, small cups or containers, dry rice in sealed sensory bags or teacher-controlled trays, green paper strips as rice plants, baskets.

**Preparation:**  
Create a simple pretend rice field area. If using real rice, keep it contained and supervise closely. Prepare paper rice plants for children to place.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children pretend to plant rice by placing green paper strips into the field mat or containers. Then they pretend to pick or collect rice plants into baskets. The teacher uses simple sequence language: “First, plant,” “Then, pick,” “Put it in the basket.”

End by showing a bowl of rice or a picture of rice and connecting it back to the field.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Focus on sequence and participation. Do not over-explain farming techniques.

### Activity 3: Tea Hill and Farm Scene

**Purpose:**  
Let children represent farms and hills through building and arrangement.

**Materials:**  
Green fabric or paper, blocks, cardboard strips, small baskets, leaves or paper leaves, farm worker figures if available, cups or pretend tea set.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare a simple hill base using blocks or folded cardboard. Place leaves, baskets, and cups nearby.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children help build a green tea hill scene. They place paper leaves on the hill, collect leaves in baskets, and move them to a small tea table or pretend tea area. The teacher repeats: “Tea leaves,” “Pick the leaves,” “Put them in the basket,” and “Tea comes from leaves.”

Children can compare rice fields and tea hills: flat/green, hill/field, food/drink.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Use tea as a cultural and plant-based material connection, not as a tasting requirement. Tasting should only happen if the school has approved it.
