# China · K3 · Lesson 1
## Chinese Characters and Writing

## 1. Lesson Identity

- Country / Place: China
- Grade: K3
- Lesson: 1 of 4

## 2. Lesson Focus

Children explore Chinese characters as a visual writing system. They notice strokes, shapes, simple picture-character connections, and try character-inspired mark-making or rubbing.

## 3. Learning Objectives

Children will:

- notice that Chinese characters are made from strokes and shapes;
- connect some simple characters with pictures or meanings;
- use brush-style marks, rubbing, tracing, or matching to explore writing visually and explain one simple idea about writing.

## 4. Teacher Language

Teacher Input:

- Look at this Chinese character.
- What can you see?
- It has lines and strokes.
- This character means mountain / sun / tree.
- Let’s trace the strokes.
- Try it slowly.
- This is writing.
- We can write a message.
- Which character do you choose?
- Put your character on the wall.

Possible Child Response:

- I see lines.
- It looks like a tree.
- This is sun / mountain / tree.
- I choose this one.
- I can write it.
- This is my character.
- It is difficult / easy.

## 5. Suggested Games and Activities

### Activity 1: Picture to Character Match

**Purpose:**  
Help children notice that some characters can be connected to simple pictures or meanings.

**Materials:**  
Picture cards and matching simple character cards, such as sun, moon, mountain, tree, person, mouth, hand, or water. Use only a small set.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare large cards with clear visuals. Place picture cards on one side and character cards on the other. Avoid using too many characters at once.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Show one picture, such as a mountain. Ask children what they see. Then show the character and invite them to notice shapes or lines. Children work in pairs or small groups to match picture cards with character cards.

**Teacher Notes:**  
This is not formal literacy instruction. The goal is visual noticing and meaning connection, not accurate memorisation or writing test performance.

### Activity 2: Big Brush Stroke Lab

**Purpose:**  
Allow children to experience writing as movement, control, and visual creation.

**Materials:**  
Large paper, water brushes, thick brushes, black paint or diluted ink substitute, trays, aprons, wipes, and sample stroke cards.

**Preparation:**  
Set up a protected table or floor area. Prepare a few simple stroke cards: horizontal line, vertical line, dot, turn, and sweep.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children practise making large strokes on paper. The teacher models slow, controlled movements. Children then choose one simple character or character-like composition to try. They can work individually or contribute to a shared “Chinese writing wall.”

**Teacher Notes:**  
Accept age-appropriate attempts. Focus on direction, control, pressure, and visual expression. Do not correct children as if this were formal calligraphy training.

### Activity 3: Character Museum Wall

**Purpose:**  
Help children organise and present their writing exploration.

**Materials:**  
Children’s brush papers, picture-character pairs, sticky labels, display board or wall space, tape, and name cards.

**Preparation:**  
Create sections on the wall, such as “Pictures,” “Characters,” and “Our Writing.” Prepare simple labels if useful.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children choose one picture-character pair or one writing piece to add to the wall. They place their work and tell a peer or teacher what they chose. Stronger children may explain the connection between picture and character.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Encourage children to speak in short, clear phrases. Allow pointing and one-word answers for children who are less confident, but invite K3 children to explain when ready.
