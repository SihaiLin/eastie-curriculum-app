# China · K3 · Lesson 2
## Paper, Silk and Porcelain

## 1. Lesson Identity

- Country / Place: China
- Grade: K3
- Lesson: 2 of 4

## 2. Lesson Focus

Children explore Chinese making through materials and patterns. They compare paper, silk-like fabric, and porcelain-style surfaces, then create simple prints, rubbings, or blue-and-white pattern designs.

## 3. Learning Objectives

Children will:

- notice different material qualities such as soft, smooth, thin, hard, light, or strong;
- explore paper, fabric, and porcelain-style visual patterns;
- use printing, rubbing, folding, or pattern-making to create a simple design and describe one material they used.

## 4. Teacher Language

Teacher Input:

- Touch the paper. How does it feel?
- This fabric is soft.
- This plate is smooth.
- Look at the blue and white pattern.
- Let’s make a pattern.
- Press and print.
- Fold the paper.
- Which material do you want to use?
- What did you make?
- Tell us about your design.

Possible Child Response:

- It is soft / smooth / hard / thin.
- I choose paper.
- I made a pattern.
- I used blue.
- This is my plate.
- I printed flowers / lines / waves.
- I like this design.

## 5. Suggested Games and Activities

### Activity 1: Material Touch and Sort

**Purpose:**  
Help children observe and compare materials before making.

**Materials:**  
Paper samples, cardboard, tissue paper, silk-like fabric or scarves, smooth ceramic-safe objects or porcelain-style images, trays, sorting mats, and label cards such as soft, smooth, rough, thin, strong.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare safe samples in small baskets. Avoid breakable real porcelain unless handled only by the teacher. Use images or plastic/ceramic-safe substitutes when needed.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children touch and compare materials. They sort them by feel or use: writing, wrapping, wearing, decorating, building, or eating from. The teacher prompts children to justify simple choices.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Keep the language concrete. K3 children can handle comparison language, but the activity should remain sensory and practical.

### Activity 2: Blue-and-White Pattern Studio

**Purpose:**  
Let children create a visual design inspired by blue-and-white porcelain patterns.

**Materials:**  
Paper plates or circular cardstock, blue markers or paint, pattern reference cards, stamps, rollers, cotton swabs, and pencils.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare pattern examples with simple motifs: dots, waves, flowers, lines, circles, borders. Avoid overly detailed designs that frustrate children.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children look at several blue-and-white pattern images and notice repeated lines, dots, flowers, or waves. They design their own paper plate using repeated marks and borders.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Frame this as pattern exploration, not copying. Children should make a design they can explain, such as “I used dots around the edge” or “I made waves.”

### Activity 3: Print, Rub, Fold

**Purpose:**  
Connect paper with making processes.

**Materials:**  
Paper, textured rubbing plates or leaves, crayons, stamps, ink pads or paint trays, simple folding paper, and drying space.

**Preparation:**  
Set up three small stations: rubbing, printing, and folding. Keep instructions visual and short.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children rotate through stations or choose one. They make a rubbing, create repeated prints, or fold paper into a simple shape. At the end, they place one piece on a “Made with Paper” display.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Do not present paper-making, silk, and porcelain as an information-heavy invention list. The lesson should stay focused on touching, comparing, making, and explaining.
