# China · K3 Overview

## 1. Country Character

China is the country children live in, and K3 children are ready to explore it not only through daily life or natural environments, but also through things people have made, built, designed, written, and used. In this unit, China can be introduced as a place of visible creation: Chinese characters, paper, silk, porcelain, traditional buildings, the Great Wall, bridges, high-speed trains, city skylines, and modern infrastructure.

The China unit should not become a dynasty-history lesson or a list of inventions to memorise. Children do not need to learn a detailed timeline or repeat adult explanations about ancient China. Instead, they should notice that people in China have created many things across time: ways of writing, useful materials, beautiful patterns, large structures, and modern transport systems. The unit should feel constructive, visual, project-based, and explanatory.

## 2. Grade-Level Lens

For K3 children, China should be approached through organising, comparing, making, explaining, and presenting. Children can compare ancient and modern creations, observe how materials are used, build structures, create visual displays, and explain one or two things they have discovered.

The main K3 lens is: **“What have people in China made, built, designed, and used?”**

K3 children may respond through drawing, building, sorting, labelling, group discussion, simple presentation, model-making, and class museum work. They can begin to explain simple relationships, such as “paper is for writing,” “a bridge helps people cross,” “a train helps people travel,” or “a wall has towers and gates.”

## 3. Classroom Setup and Key Elements

The classroom can include a Created China corner with Chinese character cards, brush-writing examples, paper and fabric samples, blue-and-white pattern images, traditional roof and gate pictures, Great Wall images, bridge photos, high-speed rail images, and Shenzhen city visuals. The setup should support making and explanation rather than only decoration.

Key elements can include:

- Chinese characters and brush marks
- paper and printing/rubbing
- silk-like fabric and texture comparison
- blue-and-white porcelain-style patterns
- the Great Wall
- traditional roofs, gates, courtyards, and bridges
- high-speed rail and modern bridges
- Shenzhen or modern city skylines

For K3, image cards, simple labels, material samples, construction materials, design templates, group display boards, and presentation spaces are more useful than long written information.

## 4. Four-Lesson Map

### Lesson 1: Chinese Characters and Writing

Children explore Chinese characters as a visible writing system. They notice strokes, shapes, picture-like origins in a simple way, and try brush-style mark-making, rubbing, tracing, matching, or character display work.

This lesson introduces writing as something people create and use to communicate.

### Lesson 2: Paper, Silk and Porcelain

Children explore Chinese making traditions through materials and patterns. They compare paper, fabric, smooth surfaces, printed marks, blue-and-white patterns, and simple craft processes.

This lesson helps children understand China through material experience and making, not through memorising invention lists.

### Lesson 3: Great Wall and Traditional Architecture

Children explore large Chinese structures and traditional building forms, including the Great Wall, towers, gates, walls, roofs, courtyards, and bridges. They build, compare, and design using blocks, Lego, cardboard, paper, or loose parts.

This lesson focuses on structure, form, function, and collaborative building.

### Lesson 4: Modern China: Trains, Bridges and Cities

Children explore modern created China through high-speed trains, bridges, metro systems, towers, and modern city scenes. They connect ancient and modern making by creating a class display or mini museum of Chinese creations.

This lesson brings the unit together through project work and simple presentation.
