# China · K3 · Lesson 3
## Great Wall and Traditional Architecture

## 1. Lesson Identity

- Country / Place: China
- Grade: K3
- Lesson: 3 of 4

## 2. Lesson Focus

Children explore Chinese structures and traditional architecture through the Great Wall, towers, gates, roofs, bridges, courtyards, and walls. They build and compare forms using construction materials.

## 3. Learning Objectives

Children will:

- notice architectural features such as walls, towers, gates, roofs, bridges, and courtyards;
- compare structure forms by height, length, shape, and function;
- build a collaborative structure model and explain one design choice, such as “This tower is tall” or “This bridge helps people cross.”

## 4. Teacher Language

Teacher Input:

- Look at the wall.
- It is long.
- This is a tower.
- This is a gate.
- This roof is curved.
- Let’s build a long wall.
- Make it strong.
- Where is the bridge?
- How can people go through?
- Tell us about your building.

Possible Child Response:

- It is long / tall / strong.
- This is a wall.
- This is a gate / tower / bridge.
- People go through here.
- I built a roof.
- Our wall is long.
- This bridge goes over the river.

## 5. Suggested Games and Activities

### Activity 1: Architecture Picture Hunt

**Purpose:**  
Help children identify visible parts of Chinese architecture before building.

**Materials:**  
Large images of the Great Wall, gates, towers, palace roofs, courtyard layouts, stone bridges, and simple feature cards: wall, tower, gate, roof, bridge.

**Preparation:**  
Select a small number of clear images. Mark or crop images so features are visible. Prepare matching feature cards.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Show one architecture image at a time. Children look for specific features: Where is the wall? Where is the gate? Is the roof straight or curved? They match feature cards to the pictures.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Keep the focus on visible form and function. Avoid turning the lesson into detailed historical explanation.

### Activity 2: Build the Long Wall

**Purpose:**  
Support collaborative construction, spatial reasoning, and structure awareness.

**Materials:**  
Large wooden blocks, Lego, cardboard bricks, small boxes, paper towers, fabric or paper landscape base, and optional small people figures.

**Preparation:**  
Set a long construction area. Prepare a simple image of the Great Wall for visual reference. Decide whether children build as one large group or in smaller teams.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children work together to build a long wall with towers and gates. The teacher prompts them to think about length, stability, pathways, and entrances. Children can add a hill, road, or small tower if time allows.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Safety matters during large construction. Keep structures at a manageable height and encourage children to negotiate space and roles.

### Activity 3: Roof, Gate and Bridge Design Challenge

**Purpose:**  
Let children explore architecture through design variation.

**Materials:**  
Blocks, magnetic tiles, cardboard strips, paper roof shapes, arches, tape, small trays, blue cloth for water, and simple design cards.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare three challenge cards: build a gate, build a curved roof, build a bridge over water. Place materials at stations.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children choose one challenge or rotate through stations. They build a gate people can pass through, a roof with a special shape, or a bridge that crosses a river. At the end, each group shows how their structure works.

**Teacher Notes:**  
This activity suits K3 because children can explain function: through, over, under, strong, long, high. Encourage explanation but do not over-correct technical vocabulary.
