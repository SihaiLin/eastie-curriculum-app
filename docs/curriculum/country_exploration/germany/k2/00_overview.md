# Germany · K2 Overview

## 1. Country Character

Germany is suitable for K2 because its strongest child-accessible elements can be sorted, compared, connected, and built: different kinds of transport, road and rail systems, forests and towns, castles and houses, clocks and simple mechanisms. The unit should make Germany feel like a place where people design routes, build structures, use machines, and live close to forests and towns.

## 2. Grade-Level Lens

For K2 children, Germany should be explored through sorting, comparing, sequencing, and simple cause-and-effect connections. Children can compare road and rail transport, sort buildings by shape and function, connect forests with animals and homes, and examine how simple moving parts work. The main lens is: “How are things built, connected, and used?”

## 3. Classroom Setup and Key Elements

The classroom can include a transport-and-building area with road tape, train tracks, vehicles, bridge pieces, blocks, gears or simple moving parts, castle and timber-house images, forest materials, and sorting cards. The environment should invite children to classify and build: road/train, town/forest, bridge/tunnel, wheel/track, house/castle/clock.

## 4. Four-Lesson Map

### Lesson 1: Roads, Cars and Trains — Sort and Compare
Children compare cars, trains, roads, tracks, bridges, and tunnels. They sort transport materials and build routes with different movement rules.

### Lesson 2: Forests, Animals and Small Towns
Children explore the connection between forests and towns: trees, paths, animals, houses, and people living near natural spaces.

### Lesson 3: Castles, Houses and Building Shapes
Children compare castles, town houses, roofs, towers, walls, windows, and bridges, then build and sort structures by visible features.

### Lesson 4: Clocks, Wheels and Simple Mechanisms
Children explore cuckoo-clock images, wheels, gears, turning parts, and simple cause-and-effect movement, then add moving or pretend-moving parts to a Germany display.
