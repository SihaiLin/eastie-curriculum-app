# Germany · K1 Overview

## 1. Country Character

Germany is a strong Country Exploration theme for young children because it can be introduced through very concrete and buildable elements: cars, trains, roads, forests, castles, small town houses, clocks, and simple story settings. It should not be presented as a list of facts. For K1, Germany works best as a place of movement, building, wheels, woods, houses, and story-like images.

## 2. Grade-Level Lens

For K1 children, Germany should be explored through seeing, touching, moving, and making. Children can push cars on road lines, build train tracks, stack blocks into castles, walk through a pretend forest, and notice simple shapes in houses and clocks. The main lens is: “What can we move, build, and notice?”

## 3. Classroom Setup and Key Elements

The classroom can include a Germany corner with toy cars, train tracks, road tape, forest fabric or paper trees, castle and small-town images, wooden blocks, wheels, bridge pieces, and simple cuckoo-clock visuals. The setup should feel hands-on and constructive rather than informational. For K1, large images, real objects, vehicle toys, blocks, and simple story props are more useful than maps or text.

## 4. Four-Lesson Map

### Lesson 1: Cars, Roads and Moving Around
Children meet Germany through cars, roads, wheels, tunnels, and movement. They push vehicles, follow road lines, and notice fast/slow, stop/go, and straight/curved roads.

### Lesson 2: Trains, Tracks and Stations
Children explore trains and tracks through building, joining pieces, making stations, and moving train toys safely along routes.

### Lesson 3: Forests, Houses and Story Paths
Children enter a simple German forest and small-town setting with trees, houses, paths, and story props. They move through a pretend forest path and build small houses.

### Lesson 4: Build a Mini Germany Town
Children combine roads, tracks, cars, trains, trees, houses, and a castle into a shared mini Germany town, then walk through and share what they made.
