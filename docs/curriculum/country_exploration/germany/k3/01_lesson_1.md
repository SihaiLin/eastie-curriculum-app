# Germany · K3 · Lesson 1
## Germany on the Move — Routes and Systems

## 1. Lesson Identity

- Country / Place: Germany
- Grade: K3
- Lesson: 1 of 4

## 2. Lesson Focus

Children explore Germany through transport systems: cars, trains, roads, railways, bridges, tunnels, stations, and routes. The focus is on organising parts into a system and explaining how people and vehicles move from one place to another.

## 3. Learning Objectives

Children will:
- organise transport elements into a simple system with routes, stations, bridges, tunnels, and destinations;
- explain how cars and trains move differently using route-based language;
- create a simple Germany transport map or model with labels, arrows, and connected paths.

## 4. Teacher Language

Teacher Input:
- A system has parts that work together.
- Where does the route start? Where does it end?
- Cars need roads. Trains need tracks.
- Add an arrow to show the way.
- Explain your route to the group.

Possible Child Response:
- start / end / route / station / bridge / tunnel
- cars go on roads / trains go on tracks
- first / next / then / because

## 5. Suggested Games and Activities

### Activity 1: Transport System Sort

**Purpose:**  
To move beyond object sorting toward system thinking.

**Materials:**  
Transport cards, route cards, station cards, bridge and tunnel images, arrows, large paper base.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare a blank route board with two destination points.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children sort transport materials into system parts: vehicle, route, stop, connector, destination. Then they place the parts on the board and add arrows to show movement.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Use the word “system” naturally but keep it concrete: parts working together.

### Activity 2: Design a Route Problem

**Purpose:**  
To encourage planning and explanation.

**Materials:**  
Blocks, road strips, tracks, bridge pieces, tunnel pieces, cars, trains, destination cards.

**Preparation:**  
Set a simple challenge: “Move people from the town to the forest” or “Connect the station to the castle.”

**How to Play / Do:**  
Small groups design a route. They decide where to place roads, tracks, bridges, or tunnels, test the route, and adjust it if vehicles cannot move smoothly.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Prompt children to explain design choices. Keep explanations short and functional.

### Activity 3: Route Map Presentation

**Purpose:**  
To support K3-level organisation and sharing.

**Materials:**  
Completed route model, labels, arrows, pointer, camera or tablet for documentation.

**Preparation:**  
Add labels for start, station, bridge, tunnel, and end.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Each group presents one part of the system. Children can say what starts here, what moves there, and what happens next.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Do not require memorised country facts. The learning is in organising and explaining the model.
