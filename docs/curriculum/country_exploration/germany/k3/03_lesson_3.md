# Germany · K3 · Lesson 3
## Forests, Stories and Cultural Imagination

## 1. Lesson Identity

- Country / Place: Germany
- Grade: K3
- Lesson: 3 of 4

## 2. Lesson Focus

Children explore Germany through forest settings and child-friendly traditional story motifs. The focus is on how place can shape imagination: paths, houses, forests, animals, characters, choices, and journeys. This is not a literary history lesson; it is a story-design and retelling experience.

## 3. Learning Objectives

Children will:
- identify forest story elements such as path, house, animal, character, problem, and destination;
- organise a simple story route using cards, props, or a floor map;
- retell or present part of a forest story through movement, puppet play, drawing, or short speech.

## 4. Teacher Language

Teacher Input:
- Where does the story start?
- Who is on the path?
- What is the problem?
- What happens next?
- Show your story route.

Possible Child Response:
- forest / path / house / character / problem
- first / next / then / finally
- he goes / she finds / they help

## 5. Suggested Games and Activities

### Activity 1: Story Element Sort

**Purpose:**  
To help children organise story components before retelling.

**Materials:**  
Cards showing forest, path, house, animal, child character, helper, object, problem, ending.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare category mats: place, character, object, problem, ending.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children sort cards into categories. Then they choose one card from each category to make the beginning of a forest story.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Keep story content gentle. Avoid frightening plots or dark imagery.

### Activity 2: Forest Story Path Map

**Purpose:**  
To connect setting with sequence.

**Materials:**  
Large paper path, tree props, house card, animal cards, character figures, arrows.

**Preparation:**  
Lay out a winding path with a clear start and end.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children place story elements along the path: start at the town, walk into the forest, meet an animal, find a house, solve a simple problem, return home. They use arrows to show order.

**Teacher Notes:**  
The goal is sequence and structure, not exact retelling of a known tale.

### Activity 3: Puppet Retell Circle

**Purpose:**  
To support expressive presentation in multiple modes.

**Materials:**  
Simple puppets, animal figures, house prop, forest backdrop, story route map.

**Preparation:**  
Choose one short class-created story path from Activity 2.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children use puppets to show one event from the story. Some children may speak; others may move a puppet or point to the next place.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Support participation at different confidence levels. Keep performance short and safe.
