# Kenya · K3 · Lesson 3
## Animal Movement and Migration Routes

## 1. Lesson Identity

- Country / Place: Kenya
- Grade: K3
- Lesson: 3 of 4

## 2. Lesson Focus

Children explore animal movement across land through simple routes and map symbols. They learn that some animals move to find water, food, and safer places. The lesson introduces migration as an idea, but does not require detailed scientific terminology or exact routes.

## 3. Learning Objectives

Children will:
- represent animal movement using routes, arrows, and sequence language;
- explain why animals may move, using simple reasons such as food, water, or space;
- create a route map or movement model showing animals travelling across a landscape.

## 4. Teacher Language

Teacher Input:
- Some animals move a long way.
- They move to find water and food.
- Start here. Go this way.
- Use arrows to show the route.
- Why do the animals move?

Possible Child Response:
- to water / to food
- start / next / end
- they go together
- arrows, route, because

## 5. Suggested Games and Activities

### Activity 1: Route Story with Arrows

**Purpose:**  
To introduce animal movement as a simple sequence.

**Materials:**  
Large landscape mat, animal group cards, arrow cards, water/grass/shade icons.

**Preparation:**  
Set up a route with a starting point, two stops, and an ending point.

**How to Play / Do:**  
The teacher tells a simple route story: “The zebras start here. They need water. They move this way.” Children place arrows and move the group cards along the route.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Keep the route short. The key idea is movement for need, not route accuracy.

### Activity 2: Design a Migration Path

**Purpose:**  
To let children organise movement routes independently or in small groups.

**Materials:**  
Blank route maps, stickers or cards, arrows, markers, animal cut-outs.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare simple map bases with land, water, and grass areas already marked.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Small groups choose an animal group and design a route from one place to another. They add arrows and stops, then explain the route: “First grass, next water, then rest.”

**Teacher Notes:**  
The map is conceptual, not geographic. Prioritise sequence and reasoning.

### Activity 3: Body Map Movement

**Purpose:**  
To connect map routes with whole-body understanding.

**Materials:**  
Floor arrows, place signs, animal headbands or cards.

**Preparation:**  
Create a large route on the floor with signs: grass, water, trees, rest.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children move as an animal group along the route. At each stop, one child explains the reason for stopping: “Water.” “Food.” “Rest.” Then the group moves to the next arrow.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Keep movement cooperative rather than competitive. A teacher can lead the first round, then children can lead later rounds.
