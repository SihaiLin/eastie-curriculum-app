# Kenya · K3 Overview

## 1. Country Character

Kenya is a strong entry point for children to organise ideas about landscapes, wildlife, habitats, animal movement, and conservation. It offers clear, child-visible relationships: grasslands support animals; animals need water and space; some animals move in groups; people observe, protect, and learn from nature. Kenya should be treated as an ecological and inquiry-based country theme, not only an “animal list.”

## 2. Grade-Level Lens

For K3, Kenya should be explored through organisation, explanation, model-making, and presentation. Children can use simple maps, categories, cause-effect language, and project roles to explain how savanna animals, land, water, movement, and protection are connected. The main lens is: “How can we organise and explain a Kenya savanna system?”

## 3. Classroom Setup and Key Elements

The classroom can include a Kenya inquiry wall with a simple map outline, savanna ecosystem images, animal research cards, waterhole and migration route visuals, binoculars or observation props, field-notes templates, and conservation signs. Materials should support group investigation, map-making, model-building, and presentation.

## 4. Four-Lesson Map

### Lesson 1: Kenya on the Map — Savanna, Mountain and Water

Children locate Kenya in a simple world context and organise major landscape elements: savanna, mountains, lakes, rivers, and parks.

### Lesson 2: Savanna Ecosystem — Animals, Needs and Relationships

Children investigate how animals connect with food, water, shelter, space, and each other in a simple savanna ecosystem.

### Lesson 3: Animal Movement and Migration Routes

Children use arrows, routes, and simple map language to represent animal movement across land toward food and water.

### Lesson 4: Kenya Wildlife Field Station

Children create a mini field station or museum display to present animals, habitats, movement routes, and ways people protect wildlife.
