# Kenya · K3 · Lesson 1
## Kenya on the Map — Savanna, Mountain and Water

## 1. Lesson Identity

- Country / Place: Kenya
- Grade: K3
- Lesson: 1 of 4

## 2. Lesson Focus

Children begin the K3 Kenya unit by organising Kenya as a place with different landscape features. They use a simple map, photos, and model materials to notice savanna, mountains, lakes, rivers, and protected wildlife areas. The emphasis is spatial organisation and landscape vocabulary, not memorising detailed geography.

## 3. Learning Objectives

Children will:
- recognise Kenya as a place in Africa using a simple map context;
- organise key landscape features such as savanna, mountain, lake, and river;
- build or annotate a simple landscape map using symbols and materials.

## 4. Teacher Language

Teacher Input:
- Kenya is in Africa.
- This is a savanna.
- Here is a mountain / lake / river.
- What can we put on the map?
- How can we show water or grassland?

Possible Child Response:
- Kenya / Africa
- savanna / mountain / lake / river
- put water here / animals here / grassland here
- labelling, placing, explaining

## 5. Suggested Games and Activities

### Activity 1: Simple Map Introduction

**Purpose:**  
To give K3 children a clear but age-appropriate spatial context for Kenya.

**Materials:**  
World map or globe, Africa map, simple Kenya outline, stickers, pointer.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare a simplified map sequence: world → Africa → Kenya. Avoid too much map detail.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Show children the world map, then Africa, then Kenya. Place a sticker on Kenya. Discuss that Kenya has cities, people, animals, grasslands, mountains, lakes, and rivers. Children take turns pointing or placing icons.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Do not test children on map memorisation. The goal is orientation and curiosity.

### Activity 2: Landscape Symbol Sorting

**Purpose:**  
To help children classify major landscape types before making a map.

**Materials:**  
Landscape photos, symbol cards for savanna, mountain, lake, river, city or park, sorting mats.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare clear photos and matching icons.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children sort photos into landscape groups. Ask: “Is this water?” “Is this high or flat?” “Where might animals find grass?” Children match each photo to a symbol.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Use comparison language: high/low, wet/dry, open/closed, flat/tall.

### Activity 3: Build a Kenya Landscape Map

**Purpose:**  
To represent landscapes visually and spatially.

**Materials:**  
Large Kenya outline or blank map base, blue paper or fabric, green/yellow paper, brown blocks or paper mountains, labels, reusable tack.

**Preparation:**  
Set up a large base on a table or floor. Prepare symbols and materials in separate trays.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children add water, grassland, mountains, and animal areas to the map. They place labels or icons and explain one choice: “The river is blue.” “Animals need grassland.”

**Teacher Notes:**  
The map can be approximate. Prioritise organisation and explanation over accuracy.
