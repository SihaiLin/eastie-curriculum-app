# Kenya · K3 · Lesson 2
## Savanna Ecosystem — Animals, Needs and Relationships

## 1. Lesson Identity

- Country / Place: Kenya
- Grade: K3
- Lesson: 2 of 4

## 2. Lesson Focus

Children investigate the Kenya savanna as a simple ecosystem. They organise animals, plants, water, shade, and space into relationships. The lesson stays age-appropriate: animals need food, water, space, and safe places; different animals use the environment in different ways.

## 3. Learning Objectives

Children will:
- identify basic parts of a savanna ecosystem, including animals, plants, water, and shelter;
- explain simple relationships such as animal-food, animal-water, and animal-space;
- create a group ecosystem diagram or model using pictures, arrows, and labels.

## 4. Teacher Language

Teacher Input:
- An ecosystem has living things and places.
- Animals need food, water, and space.
- What does this animal need?
- How are these connected?
- Use an arrow to show the connection.

Possible Child Response:
- animal needs water
- zebra eats grass
- giraffe eats leaves
- connected / arrow / because
- drawing, labelling, explaining

## 5. Suggested Games and Activities

### Activity 1: Ecosystem Parts Investigation

**Purpose:**  
To introduce ecosystem thinking through concrete parts.

**Materials:**  
Cards for animals, grass, trees, water, sun, rocks, shade, people/park ranger if appropriate; sorting mats.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare two or three category mats: living things, places, needs.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children sort ecosystem cards. Discuss how each part helps animals live. The teacher introduces simple connections: “Grass helps zebra.” “Trees help giraffe.” “Water helps many animals.”

**Teacher Notes:**  
Avoid complex food-web diagrams. K3 can use arrows, but the logic should remain simple and visible.

### Activity 2: Connection Arrows

**Purpose:**  
To help children represent relationships visually.

**Materials:**  
Animal cards, need cards, arrow cards, large board or floor mat.

**Preparation:**  
Place animal cards on one side and need/place cards on the other.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children choose an animal and connect it to a need using an arrow. They explain the connection with a short phrase: “Elephant needs water.” “Giraffe eats leaves.”

**Teacher Notes:**  
Encourage “because” for children who are ready, but do not require full sentences from everyone.

### Activity 3: Build an Ecosystem Model

**Purpose:**  
To turn relationships into a collaborative physical model.

**Materials:**  
Large base, animal figures, paper grass, trees, blue fabric, labels, arrows, blocks, small signs.

**Preparation:**  
Divide the model space into simple zones: water, grassland, trees, resting area.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Small groups add one zone or one animal group to the model. Each group places arrows or labels showing one relationship. The class reviews the model together.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Assign roles if needed: builder, card placer, label helper, speaker. This keeps group work organised.
