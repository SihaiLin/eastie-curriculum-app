# Kenya · K2 · Lesson 4
## Kenya Savanna Sorting Map

## 1. Lesson Identity

- Country / Place: Kenya
- Grade: K2
- Lesson: 4 of 4

## 2. Lesson Focus

Children organise what they have explored about Kenya into a collaborative savanna sorting map. The map includes animals, features, needs, habitats, and movement paths. The focus is categorisation and simple explanation, not accurate geography.

## 3. Learning Objectives

Children will:
- organise Kenya animal and environment materials into clear groups on a shared map;
- use simple relationships such as animal-feature, animal-need, and animal-place;
- participate in a group review by placing, pointing, or explaining one part of the map.

## 4. Teacher Language

Teacher Input:
- Let’s make a savanna map.
- Put animals here.
- Put water here.
- Which animals have stripes?
- Show me one animal and one place.

Possible Child Response:
- zebra has stripes
- elephant needs water
- giraffe by tree
- sorting, grouping, pointing, short explanation

## 5. Suggested Games and Activities

### Activity 1: Sort Before We Build

**Purpose:**  
To help children organise materials before creating the final map.

**Materials:**  
Animal cards, feature cards, need cards, habitat cards, baskets or trays.

**Preparation:**  
Set out sorting baskets labelled with simple icons: animals, patterns, needs, places.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children sort materials into baskets. The teacher checks categories with simple questions: “Is this an animal?” “Is this water?” “Does this show stripes?”

**Teacher Notes:**  
This prepares children for a clearer final display and prevents random placement.

### Activity 2: Build the Savanna Map

**Purpose:**  
To create a shared visual organisation of the Kenya unit.

**Materials:**  
Large paper or fabric base, blue paper for water, green/yellow/brown paper, animal cards, arrows, glue or reusable tack.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare a large base with enough space for water, grass, trees, and animal groups.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children place landscape materials first, then animals, then arrows or feature labels. The teacher supports simple reasoning: “Zebra near grass.” “Animals go to water.” “Stripes group here.”

**Teacher Notes:**  
The map should show relationships, not just decoration. Ask children why they placed something where they did, but accept short answers.

### Activity 3: One Thing I Know

**Purpose:**  
To give children a simple way to share understanding from the unit.

**Materials:**  
Completed savanna map, pointer stick, animal cards.

**Preparation:**  
Arrange children in a small group around the map.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Each child chooses one thing to show: an animal, a need, a place, or a path. The teacher helps them say or demonstrate one idea: “Zebra has stripes.” “Elephant needs water.” “Animals go together.”

**Teacher Notes:**  
Sharing can be individual or pair-based. Do not turn it into a formal presentation.
