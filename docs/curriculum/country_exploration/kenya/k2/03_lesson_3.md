# Kenya · K2 · Lesson 3
## Moving Across the Grassland

## 1. Lesson Identity

- Country / Place: Kenya
- Grade: K2
- Lesson: 3 of 4

## 2. Lesson Focus

Children explore movement across the Kenya grassland using paths, arrows, animal groups, and waterholes. The lesson introduces a simple version of animal travel and migration: animals may move to find food, water, and safe places.

## 3. Learning Objectives

Children will:
- use paths and arrows to show animal movement across a grassland scene;
- understand that some animals move in groups to find food or water;
- create and explain a simple animal travel path using cards, figures, or body movement.

## 4. Teacher Language

Teacher Input:
- The animals move.
- They go to water.
- They go together.
- Follow the path.
- Where do they go next?

Possible Child Response:
- water / grass / together
- go here / next / path
- moving figures, placing arrows, following routes

## 5. Suggested Games and Activities

### Activity 1: Follow the Animal Path

**Purpose:**  
To make animal travel concrete through body movement.

**Materials:**  
Floor arrows, animal cards, waterhole image, grass image, resting-place image.

**Preparation:**  
Create a simple route on the floor: grass → water → shade → rest.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children follow the arrows while holding animal cards. At each stop, the teacher names the place and the reason: “Water. The zebra drinks.” “Shade. The animal rests.”

**Teacher Notes:**  
Use predictable, calm movement. Keep the path short enough for children to complete successfully.

### Activity 2: Animal Group Travel

**Purpose:**  
To introduce the idea of animals moving together.

**Materials:**  
Multiple zebra or wildebeest cards, animal figures, large mat, arrow cards.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare a grassland mat with two or three destination points.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children move a group of animals together from one place to another. They add arrows to show direction. The teacher asks, “Do they go alone or together?” Children answer by placing one animal or many animals.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Do not require the term “migration.” The idea is enough: animals move together to find what they need.

### Activity 3: Make a Travel Strip

**Purpose:**  
To help children sequence movement from first to next to last.

**Materials:**  
Long paper strips, stickers or small pictures of grass, water, trees, animals, arrow stickers, glue.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare one strip per child or per small group.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children arrange pictures on a strip to show where an animal goes first, next, and last. They can place arrows between pictures. The teacher supports simple narration: “First grass, next water, last tree.”

**Teacher Notes:**  
This activity links Country Exploration with early sequencing. Keep sequence language simple and visual.
