# Kenya · K2 · Lesson 2
## Animal Homes and Needs

## 1. Lesson Identity

- Country / Place: Kenya
- Grade: K2
- Lesson: 2 of 4

## 2. Lesson Focus

Children explore how savanna animals relate to their environment. They connect animals with grass, trees, water, shade, and resting places. The lesson introduces simple ecological relationships without becoming a science lecture.

## 3. Learning Objectives

Children will:
- identify simple environmental elements in a Kenya savanna scene, such as grass, trees, and water;
- match animals with basic needs or places using cards and small-world materials;
- build a simple habitat scene that shows animals and their environment together.

## 4. Teacher Language

Teacher Input:
- Animals need water.
- The giraffe eats leaves.
- The zebra eats grass.
- Put the animal near the water.
- Where can the animal rest?

Possible Child Response:
- water / grass / tree
- zebra eats grass
- giraffe near tree
- matching, placing, building

## 5. Suggested Games and Activities

### Activity 1: What Do Animals Need?

**Purpose:**  
To connect animals with basic needs in a concrete way.

**Materials:**  
Animal cards, need cards showing water, grass, leaves, shade, resting place.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare cards with simple pictures rather than text-heavy labels.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children choose an animal and match it with one or two need cards. The teacher models simple statements: “Zebra needs grass.” “Elephant needs water.”

**Teacher Notes:**  
Keep relationships broad and simple. Avoid predator-prey details unless children raise them naturally, and handle them lightly.

### Activity 2: Build a Waterhole

**Purpose:**  
To help children understand the waterhole as a shared place for animals.

**Materials:**  
Blue fabric or paper, animal figures, blocks, paper grass, trees, stones.

**Preparation:**  
Set up a blank savanna mat with a blue “waterhole” in the centre.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children add grass, trees, rocks, and animals around the waterhole. Ask where animals can drink, stand, rest, or hide. Children place animals and explain with short phrases.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Emphasise shared space and care. Do not create fighting or hunting scenarios as the main play direction.

### Activity 3: Habitat Match Relay

**Purpose:**  
To combine movement, matching, and animal-environment relationships.

**Materials:**  
Animal cards on one side of the room, habitat/need cards on the other side.

**Preparation:**  
Place cards clearly and limit the number of animals to avoid crowding.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children pick an animal card, walk to the habitat side, and match it with a need card. They return and show the group: “Elephant — water.” “Giraffe — tree.”

**Teacher Notes:**  
Use walking, not running. The activity should support thinking, not speed competition.
