# Kenya · K2 Overview

## 1. Country Character

Kenya is a strong nature-and-wildlife country theme. Its most accessible content for young children is the open savanna, animal groups, movement, migration, patterns, and the relationship between animals and habitats. Kenya should not be reduced to “many animals.” It should help children notice how grassland animals live, move, group, and depend on the environment.

## 2. Grade-Level Lens

For K2, Kenya should be explored through sorting, comparing, matching, and simple relationship-building. Children can compare animal body features, group animals by movement or habitat, connect animals with water/grass/trees, and understand a simple version of animal movement across the land. The main lens is: “How are animals different? What do they need? Where do they move?”

## 3. Classroom Setup and Key Elements

The classroom can include a Kenya savanna board with animal groups, habitat zones, waterhole images, grassland colours, animal tracks, migration arrows, pattern samples, and sorting baskets. K2 materials should invite classification and comparison: animal cards, habitat cards, movement cards, size cards, footprint cards, and small-world maps.

## 4. Four-Lesson Map

### Lesson 1: Savanna Animals — Same and Different

Children compare giraffes, zebras, elephants, lions, and other savanna animals by size, body parts, patterns, and movement.

### Lesson 2: Animal Homes and Needs

Children connect animals with grass, trees, water, shade, and resting places. They build simple habitat scenes and match animals to what they need.

### Lesson 3: Moving Across the Grassland

Children explore simple animal movement and migration ideas through paths, arrows, waterholes, and group travel.

### Lesson 4: Kenya Savanna Sorting Map

Children create a collaborative map that organises animals, needs, patterns, and movement paths into one Kenya savanna display.
