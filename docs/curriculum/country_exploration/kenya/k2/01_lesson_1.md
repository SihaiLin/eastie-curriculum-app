# Kenya · K2 · Lesson 1
## Savanna Animals — Same and Different

## 1. Lesson Identity

- Country / Place: Kenya
- Grade: K2
- Lesson: 1 of 4

## 2. Lesson Focus

Children explore Kenya through savanna animals and compare visible features. The focus is not memorising animal facts, but noticing same and different: tall neck, long trunk, stripes, spots, mane, big body, small body, fast runner, slow walker.

## 3. Learning Objectives

Children will:
- compare selected Kenya savanna animals by visible features and movement;
- sort animal cards using simple categories such as big/small, stripes/spots, or walk/run;
- use short phrases, gestures, or placement choices to explain similarities and differences.

## 4. Teacher Language

Teacher Input:
- What is the same?
- What is different?
- This animal has stripes.
- This animal has a long neck.
- Put the big animals here.

Possible Child Response:
- same / different
- big elephant / tall giraffe / zebra stripes
- sorting, matching, pointing, short phrases

## 5. Suggested Games and Activities

### Activity 1: Animal Feature Circle

**Purpose:**  
To help children notice visible animal features before sorting.

**Materials:**  
Large animal cards: giraffe, zebra, elephant, lion, cheetah, ostrich; feature cards such as stripes, spots, long neck, trunk, mane.

**Preparation:**  
Place animal cards in a circle and keep feature cards in a small basket.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children choose one feature card and place it beside an animal that matches. The teacher asks simple comparison questions: “Who has stripes?” “Who is tall?” “Who has a trunk?”

**Teacher Notes:**  
Use real photos or realistic illustrations when possible. K2 can compare more accurately when images are clear.

### Activity 2: Same-Different Sorting Mats

**Purpose:**  
To practise classification using child-friendly categories.

**Materials:**  
Sorting mats labelled with icons, animal cards, baskets.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare two or three simple category pairs: big/small, stripes/spots, walk/run.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children sort animal cards onto mats. After sorting, invite them to show one choice: “Zebra here — stripes.” “Elephant here — big.”

**Teacher Notes:**  
Do not use too many categories at once. Change the sorting rule only after children understand the first rule.

### Activity 3: Animal Comparison Line

**Purpose:**  
To make comparison physical and collaborative.

**Materials:**  
Animal cards, floor line, clips or standing card holders.

**Preparation:**  
Mark a simple line on the floor. One side can mean “tall,” the other “short,” or one side “fast,” the other “slow.”

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children place animal cards along the line based on the teacher’s prompt. For example, giraffe near “tall,” lion or cheetah near “fast,” elephant near “big.” Discuss the placements briefly.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Accept approximate reasoning. The point is to explain and compare, not to rank animals scientifically.
