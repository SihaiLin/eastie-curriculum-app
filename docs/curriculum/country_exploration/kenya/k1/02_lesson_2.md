# Kenya · K1 · Lesson 2
## Animal Moves and Footprints

## 1. Lesson Identity

- Country / Place: Kenya
- Grade: K1
- Lesson: 2 of 4

## 2. Lesson Focus

Children explore animal movement and footprints through gross-motor play, matching, and simple pretend exploration. The lesson connects animals with how they move, where they walk, and the marks they may leave.

## 3. Learning Objectives

Children will:
- imitate safe animal movements such as stomp, stretch, walk, tiptoe, and rest;
- match simple footprint shapes with familiar Kenya animal pictures;
- join a group movement game with teacher support and clear boundaries.

## 4. Teacher Language

Teacher Input:
- Walk slowly.
- Stomp like an elephant.
- Stretch tall like a giraffe.
- Look, footprints!
- Which animal made this footprint?

Possible Child Response:
- elephant / giraffe / zebra / lion
- stomp / walk / tall
- matching, pointing, stepping, copying movement

## 5. Suggested Games and Activities

### Activity 1: Footprint Trail

**Purpose:**  
To help children notice that animals move through space and may leave tracks.

**Materials:**  
Large laminated footprint cards, animal cards, tape, floor space.

**Preparation:**  
Create a simple footprint trail on the floor. Put an animal card at the end of each trail.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children follow the footprints slowly. At the end of the trail, they find an animal card and name, point to, or imitate the animal. Repeat with different footprint sizes or shapes.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Footprints do not need to be scientifically exact. They are a child-friendly prompt for movement and matching.

### Activity 2: Move and Freeze

**Purpose:**  
To practise controlled movement while connecting animals with action words.

**Materials:**  
Animal cards, drum or shaker, open movement area.

**Preparation:**  
Clear the floor and mark a boundary for movement.

**How to Play / Do:**  
The teacher shows an animal card and gives a movement cue. Children move while the drum or shaker plays. When the sound stops, everyone freezes like the animal.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Use soft volume and predictable stop-start rhythm. The aim is body control, not speed.

### Activity 3: Match the Animal Home Path

**Purpose:**  
To connect movement, animals, and simple places in the grassland.

**Materials:**  
Animal figures, footprint cards, paper tree, paper grass, rock blocks, mat.

**Preparation:**  
Set up a small grassland mat with simple destinations: tree, grass, rock, resting place.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children choose an animal and move it along a footprint path to a place. The teacher narrates: “The giraffe walks to the tree.” “The elephant walks to the water.” Children can repeat a word, point, or simply place the animal.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Do not require correct habitat knowledge at K1 level. Focus on careful handling and simple action-language connection.
