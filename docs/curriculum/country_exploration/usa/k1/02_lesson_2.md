# USA · K1 · Lesson 2
## USA Food and Everyday Pop Culture

## 1. Lesson Identity

- Country / Place: USA
- Grade: K1
- Lesson: 2 of 4

## 2. Lesson Focus

Children explore familiar USA-related food and everyday pop-culture images through pretend play, matching, sorting, and simple choice-making.

## 3. Learning Objectives

Children will:
- recognise or explore familiar food items linked with USA popular culture;
- sort, choose, set, or pretend with food props;
- connect food, pictures, and simple social routines through playful exploration without needing to explain brands or cultural history.

## 4. Teacher Language

Teacher Input:
- Look at the food.
- This is a hamburger.
- This is popcorn.
- Do you like it?
- I like hamburgers.
- Put it on the plate.
- Here you are.
- Thank you.

Possible Child Response:
- hamburger
- popcorn
- fries
- drink
- I like it.
- Here you are.
- Thank you.

## 5. Suggested Games and Activities

### Activity 1: USA Food Basket Sort

**Purpose:**  
Help children recognise and sort familiar food props.

**Materials:**  
Toy food or picture cards: hamburger, fries, hot dog, popcorn, drink cup, apple or other contrast foods; baskets or trays.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare two or three baskets: “food,” “drink,” and “not today” or “I like.” Use pictures if toy food is not available.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Show one food item at a time and invite children to place it into a basket. Keep the sorting rule very simple: food/drink, big/small, or I like/I don’t like. Repeat the names naturally without requiring full sentences.

**Teacher Notes:**  
This is not a nutrition lesson and not a brand lesson. Keep the focus on familiar food objects, choosing, sorting, and pretend play.

### Activity 2: Popcorn Cinema Corner

**Purpose:**  
Use popcorn and cinema-style play to introduce a child-friendly pop-culture setting.

**Materials:**  
Popcorn picture cards or paper popcorn crafts, paper cups, pretend tickets, chairs arranged like a small cinema, short silent cartoon or image slideshow if appropriate.

**Preparation:**  
Set up a small “cinema” area. Prepare simple tickets and cups.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children take a ticket, sit in the cinema corner, and receive a paper popcorn cup. The teacher models: “Ticket, please,” “Here you are,” “Thank you,” and “Popcorn.” Children watch a very short visual clip or look at a slideshow of USA pop-culture-style images such as cartoons, sports, or city scenes.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Keep screen time short if used. The main experience is role play and routine language, not watching a long video.

### Activity 3: Hamburger Shop Role Play

**Purpose:**  
Let children connect USA food elements with social interaction and pretend service routines.

**Materials:**  
Toy hamburgers or paper hamburger parts, plates, trays, simple shop sign, pretend money if desired.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare a small table as a hamburger shop. Place food items in baskets.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children take turns being shop helper and customer. The helper gives a hamburger, fries, or drink. The teacher models short exchanges: “A hamburger, please,” “Here you are,” “Thank you.” Children can also build a paper hamburger by stacking bun, lettuce, cheese, and patty shapes.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Use commercial brand images carefully. If shown, frame them as examples of familiar pop-culture symbols, not advertising.
