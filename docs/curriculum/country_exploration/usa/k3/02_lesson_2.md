# USA · K3 · Lesson 2
## USA Icons — Food, Sports and Popular Culture

## 1. Lesson Identity

- Country / Place: USA
- Grade: K3
- Lesson: 2 of 4

## 2. Lesson Focus

Children investigate familiar USA icons from food, sports, film, cartoons, and everyday visual culture, then discuss why some images become widely recognised.

## 3. Learning Objectives

Children will:
- identify familiar USA-linked food, sport, and pop-culture images;
- organise icons by category and function;
- compare what feels familiar and what feels new, then select one icon for a USA guide or museum display.

## 4. Teacher Language

Teacher Input:
- This is an icon.
- Many people recognise it.
- Is it food, sport, or media?
- Where have you seen this before?
- Why do people remember this picture?
- Choose one icon for your guide.
- What does it show about the USA?

Possible Child Response:
- It is food.
- It is sport.
- I have seen this before.
- Many people know it.
- I choose basketball.
- It shows popular culture.

## 5. Suggested Games and Activities

### Activity 1: Familiar or New?

**Purpose:**  
Help children connect prior experience with new country exploration.

**Materials:**  
Image cards: hamburger, hot dog, popcorn, basketball, baseball, Hollywood sign, cartoon/superhero-style images, Coca-Cola-style product image, yellow taxi, Statue of Liberty.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare two floor mats: “I have seen this” and “New to me.”

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children choose image cards and place them on one of the two mats. Discuss which images many children know and which are new. The teacher introduces the idea that some pictures become widely recognised around the world.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Use commercial products as cultural examples only. Avoid making the activity feel like brand promotion.

### Activity 2: Icon Category Challenge

**Purpose:**  
Strengthen organisation and explanation using concrete categories.

**Materials:**  
Icon cards, category labels: Food, Sport, Film / Cartoon, City, Landmark, Art.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare a group table with category labels. Include a few cards that could fit more than one category.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children sort cards into categories, then choose one card and explain why it belongs there. For example: “Basketball is sport,” “Popcorn is food,” “Hollywood is film.” Stronger children can suggest two possible categories.

**Teacher Notes:**  
K3 children can begin to understand “icon” as “a picture or thing many people recognise.” Keep the definition simple.

### Activity 3: Choose One USA Icon Page

**Purpose:**  
Prepare an individual or group page for the final mini guide or museum.

**Materials:**  
Blank guide page templates, icon cards, drawing materials, glue, simple sentence frames.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare page templates with spaces for picture, name, category, and one sentence.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Each child or group chooses one USA icon. They draw or paste the image, write or copy the name, select a category, and complete a short sentence with teacher support, such as “This is food,” “This is sport,” or “Many people know this.”

**Teacher Notes:**  
Children do not need to write independently. Teacher-scribed explanations are acceptable.
