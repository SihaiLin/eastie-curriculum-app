# USA · K3 · Lesson 4
## Create and Present a USA Mini Museum

## 1. Lesson Identity

- Country / Place: USA
- Grade: K3
- Lesson: 4 of 4

## 2. Lesson Focus

Children organise their USA discoveries into a mini museum or mini guide, then present one selected part to classmates.

## 3. Learning Objectives

Children will:
- organise USA learning into categories such as map, city, landmark, food, sport, and art;
- select and arrange images, models, labels, or artwork for a shared display;
- present one discovery and understand that a country can be introduced through multiple types of information.

## 4. Teacher Language

Teacher Input:
- Let’s make a USA mini museum.
- Which category is this?
- What does your part show?
- Why did you choose it?
- Please present your work.
- This part shows city life.
- This part shows food.
- Thank you for sharing.

Possible Child Response:
- This is our USA museum.
- This part shows landmarks.
- I chose the taxi.
- It is transport.
- I made a Pop Art poster.
- I like this because it is bright.

## 5. Suggested Games and Activities

### Activity 1: Museum Category Setup

**Purpose:**  
Help children organise the final display clearly.

**Materials:**  
Large boards or tables, category labels, children’s work from previous lessons, picture cards, models, guide pages, tape or stands.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare display zones: Map, City and Landmarks, Food and Sports, Pop Art and Visual Culture. Adjust categories based on actual class work.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children help place work into the correct display zones. They decide where guide pages, pictures, buildings, posters, and cards should go. The teacher prompts them to explain: “Why does this go here?”

**Teacher Notes:**  
The final display should show children’s thinking and organisation. It does not need to look like a polished adult exhibition.

### Activity 2: Curator Practice

**Purpose:**  
Prepare children to present one part of the mini museum.

**Materials:**  
Display items, pointer sticks, simple speaking cards, name tags if desired.

**Preparation:**  
Assign each child or group one display item or category to introduce.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children practise standing near their chosen item and saying one or two simple sentences. The teacher models: “This is a landmark. It is the Statue of Liberty.” Children then practise with a partner or teacher.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Use “curator” as a teacher-facing idea if needed. Children can simply understand that they are museum helpers showing visitors around.

### Activity 3: USA Mini Museum Walk

**Purpose:**  
Give children a meaningful project-sharing experience.

**Materials:**  
Completed mini museum, camera for documentation, optional visitor stickers or feedback cards.

**Preparation:**  
Arrange a safe route through the mini museum. Invite another class or a small group of peers if appropriate.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children take turns presenting one display item. Visitors walk through the museum and listen. Presenters point, name, or explain their chosen item. At the end, children choose one favourite discovery and place a sticker or card nearby.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Keep presentations short. The goal is confidence, organisation, and simple explanation, not memorised speeches.
