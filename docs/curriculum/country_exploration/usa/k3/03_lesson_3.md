# USA · K3 · Lesson 3
## Pop Art and Visual Communication

## 1. Lesson Identity

- Country / Place: USA
- Grade: K3
- Lesson: 3 of 4

## 2. Lesson Focus

Children explore Pop Art-inspired visual communication by repeating, enlarging, recolouring, and arranging familiar images to make a strong visual message.

## 3. Learning Objectives

Children will:
- understand that repeated and colourful images can communicate strongly;
- identify same/different, repetition, enlargement, and colour change in visual examples;
- design a Pop Art-inspired page or poster using a USA-related icon and explain one design choice in simple language.

## 4. Teacher Language

Teacher Input:
- The image repeats.
- The colour changes.
- This picture is big and bright.
- What message does it give?
- Choose one image.
- Repeat it four times.
- What did you change?
- Tell us about your design.

Possible Child Response:
- I changed the colour.
- I repeated the picture.
- It is bright.
- I chose a taxi.
- My picture shows food.
- Same picture, different colours.

## 5. Suggested Games and Activities

### Activity 1: Visual Message Detective

**Purpose:**  
Help children notice how images can attract attention through repetition, colour, and size.

**Materials:**  
Teacher-made Pop Art-style examples using child-appropriate images: hamburger, taxi, basketball, star, building, drink bottle shape.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare two or three clear examples. Avoid overly complex or adult-oriented art discussion.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children look at the examples and identify what repeats, what changes, and what is big or bright. The teacher asks, “What do you notice?” “Which picture repeats?” “What colour changed?”

**Teacher Notes:**  
This is not an art history lecture. Keep the focus on visual communication and design choices.

### Activity 2: USA Pop Poster Design

**Purpose:**  
Let children create a designed output for the final USA display.

**Materials:**  
Poster paper or four-square templates, markers, coloured paper, stamps, icon templates, glue, scissors, repeated-image examples.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare image templates and sentence frames such as “I chose ___” and “I changed ___.”

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children choose one USA-related icon and repeat it across a page or poster. They change colour, background, size, or arrangement. Encourage them to make the image visually strong and easy to recognise.

**Teacher Notes:**  
K3 children can make more intentional design choices than K1/K2. Encourage planning before sticking or colouring.

### Activity 3: Design Choice Share

**Purpose:**  
Prepare children for final presentation by explaining one visual decision.

**Materials:**  
Finished posters, sentence cards, display wall.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare simple speaking frames:
- I chose ___ .
- I repeated ___ .
- I changed ___ .
- My picture shows ___ .

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children show their poster to a partner or small group and use one sentence frame to explain their design. Then they place the poster on the USA information wall or mini museum area.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Accept incomplete speech and teacher-supported explanation. The goal is clear communication, not perfect language.
