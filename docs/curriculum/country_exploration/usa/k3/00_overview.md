# USA · K3 Overview

## 1. Country Character

The USA is a modern country theme with strong visual, cultural, and urban entry points. It can be explored through famous landmarks, large cities, popular food, sports, visual culture, and invention or media-related imagery. Children may already know some USA-linked elements from everyday life: hamburgers, basketball, cartoons, superheroes, Hollywood-style film culture, yellow taxis, bridges, and the Statue of Liberty.

For K3 children, the USA can become more than a set of familiar symbols. It can be used for organising information, comparing categories, making simple explanations, and creating a small guide or museum. The unit should still stay concrete, but K3 children can begin to ask: What kind of place is this? What symbols help people recognise it? How can we introduce it to others?

## 2. Grade-Level Lens

K3 children explore the USA through project-based investigation, information organisation, simple explanation, and presentation. The main lens is: **How can we organise and present what we know about this country?**

K3 can work with categories such as city, landmark, food, sport, art, media, and history-light symbols. They can create a mini guide, discovery board, or classroom museum. The goal is not formal geography or politics, but the ability to select information, group it meaningfully, design an output, and explain it to classmates.

## 3. Classroom Setup and Key Elements

The classroom can be arranged as a USA research and design studio. Suggested areas include: a city and landmarks wall, a pop culture and food table, a sports and movement corner, a Pop Art design area, and a mini guide or museum-making station.

For K3, the classroom should support children in organising information. Labels, category cards, maps, simple timelines, image sets, blank guide pages, poster templates, and construction materials are useful. More advanced elements such as Hollywood, Mayflower, NASA, or national parks can be introduced selectively, but should not overload the unit.

Key elements: USA map / New York skyline / Statue of Liberty / Golden Gate Bridge / yellow taxi / hamburger / basketball / Hollywood / Pop Art / national parks or NASA as optional extensions

## 4. Four-Lesson Map

### Lesson 1: Where Is the USA? Cities, Maps and Landmarks

Children locate the USA in a simple world context and organise major visual symbols into categories such as map, city, landmark, and transport. They begin building a shared USA information wall.

### Lesson 2: USA Icons — Food, Sports and Popular Culture

Children investigate familiar USA icons such as hamburgers, popcorn, basketball, baseball, Hollywood-style media, cartoons, and superhero visuals. They discuss why some images become widely recognised.

### Lesson 3: Pop Art and Visual Communication

Children explore Pop Art as a way images can be repeated, enlarged, recoloured, and turned into strong visual messages. They create a designed page or poster for the USA mini guide.

### Lesson 4: Create and Present a USA Mini Museum

Children organise their work into a USA mini museum or mini guide. They present one chosen part, explaining what it shows and why they included it.
