# USA · K3 · Lesson 1
## Where Is the USA? Cities, Maps and Landmarks

## 1. Lesson Identity

- Country / Place: USA
- Grade: K3
- Lesson: 1 of 4

## 2. Lesson Focus

Children explore the USA through a simple world-map context, then organise major city, landmark, transport, and symbol images into categories.

## 3. Learning Objectives

Children will:
- understand that the USA is a large country in North America at a simple map level;
- identify several recognisable USA symbols such as the Statue of Liberty, city skylines, bridges, and yellow taxis;
- organise images into categories and begin preparing information for a later USA mini guide or mini museum.

## 4. Teacher Language

Teacher Input:
- This is the USA.
- The USA is in North America.
- What can you see in this city?
- This is a landmark.
- Let’s sort the pictures.
- Which pictures show transport?
- Which pictures show landmarks?
- Why did you put it there?

Possible Child Response:
- This is the USA.
- It is a city.
- It is a landmark.
- I see a bridge.
- I put it here because it is transport.
- The taxi goes in the city group.

## 5. Suggested Games and Activities

### Activity 1: Map to Symbol

**Purpose:**  
Introduce the USA through a simple geographic context without turning the lesson into memorisation.

**Materials:**  
World map or globe, simple USA outline map, China marker, USA marker, picture cards of USA symbols.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare a large, simple map. Mark China and the USA clearly.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Show China and the USA on the map. Briefly explain that the class is exploring the USA. Place the USA symbol cards around the map. Children choose a symbol and place it near the USA map, saying or hearing what it is.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Do not require children to memorise states, capitals, or exact locations. Map work should support orientation only.

### Activity 2: USA Category Sort

**Purpose:**  
Build K3 information organisation skills.

**Materials:**  
Picture cards: USA map, Statue of Liberty, Golden Gate Bridge, skyline, yellow taxi, hamburger, basketball, Pop Art image, Hollywood sign; category headers: Map, City, Landmark, Food, Sport, Art / Media.

**Preparation:**  
Prepare a sorting board or floor mat with categories.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children work in small groups to sort images. They discuss where each card belongs. The teacher asks follow-up questions: “Why is this a landmark?” “Could this go in another group?” Children place cards and explain choices with support.

**Teacher Notes:**  
Allow flexible thinking. Some images, such as Golden Gate Bridge, can be both landmark and city/transport-related.

### Activity 3: Start the USA Information Wall

**Purpose:**  
Begin a continuing project output that can be developed across all four lessons.

**Materials:**  
Large display paper, category labels, sticky notes, markers, picture cards, tape.

**Preparation:**  
Set up an empty USA information wall with category spaces.

**How to Play / Do:**  
Children transfer selected sorted images to the information wall. Each group adds one short label, drawing, or teacher-scribed sentence. Examples: “This is a landmark,” “A taxi is transport,” “This city has tall buildings.”

**Teacher Notes:**  
K3 children can contribute short written or oral explanations, but the teacher may scribe when needed.
